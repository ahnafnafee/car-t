## Highlights

- •
Out-of-specification (OOS) product that does not meet at least one of the commercial release criteria has been provided in a [clinical trial](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/medicine-and-dentistry/clinical-trial).

- •
OOS tisagenlecleucel had comparable outcomes to in-specification products in both clinical and real-world studies.

- •
Infusion of OOS tisagenlecleucel is feasible, safe, and a viable option for patients who have limited therapeutic options.


## Abstract

### Background

The final manufactured tisagenlecleucel product should meet the commercial product release specifications to ensure the quality in terms of safety, purity, identity, and potency. However, it may occasionally fail to meet these specifications due to the nature of patient-derived cells with variable properties as starting material and the complex manufacturing process. The final product that does not meet at least one of the commercial release specifications is referred to as “out-of-specification” (OOS). However, the benefit-risk profile of OOS tisagenlecleucel has not yet been fully elucidated.

### Aims

To evaluate the safety and efficacy of OOS tisagenlecleucel in Japanese patients with relapsed or refractory (r/r) diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) and B-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia (B-ALL).

### Methods

This is a single-arm, open-label, multicenter phase 3b study (NCT04094311). Patients consistent with label indication were enrolled and followed-up for 3 months.

### Results

Of the 29 patients enrolled between December 2019 and May 2022 across 13 qualified sites in Japan, 28 received tisagenlecleucel, and of these, 23 had r/r DLBCL and 5 had r/r B-ALL. The primary reasons for OOS were low cell viability (15 of 24 batches) and low dose (8 of 23 batches) tisagenlecleucel in the r/r DLBCL group, and high dose (4 of 5 batches) in the r/r B-ALL group. In patients with r/r DLBCL, the grade 3 or 4 cytokine release syndrome and immune effector cell-associated neurotoxicity syndrome occurred in 3 and 1 patients, respectively. Response assessments were performed for 15 of 23 patients with r/r DLBCL: 6 achieved a complete response, and 1 achieved a partial response as the best response within 3 months.

### Conclusions

Despite the limited patient sample size, our findings affirm that the infusion of OOS tisagenlecleucel is a viable option, with no observed increase in toxicity and outcomes comparable to those of in-specification products in clinical and real-world studies.

## Introduction

[Chimeric antigen receptor](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/pharmacology-toxicology-and-pharmaceutical-science/chimeric-antigen-receptor) (CAR) T-cell therapy has revolutionized the treatment of [hematological malignancies](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/pharmacology-toxicology-and-pharmaceutical-science/hematologic-malignancy) \[ [1](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1465324925006851#bib0001)\]. Tisagenlecleucel, an autologous anti-CD19 [CAR](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/medicine-and-dentistry/chimeric-antigen-receptor) T-cell therapy, has demonstrated high response rates in [clinical trials](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/medicine-and-dentistry/clinical-trial) and has been approved for [pediatric](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/medicine-and-dentistry/pediatrics) and young adults (aged ≤25 years) with relapsed or refractory (r/r) B-cell [acute lymphoblastic leukemia](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/pharmacology-toxicology-and-pharmaceutical-science/acute-lymphoblastic-leukemia) (B-ALL), adults with r/r diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) and adult patients with r/r [follicular lymphoma](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/medicine-and-dentistry/follicular-lymphoma) (FL) worldwide, including in Japan \[ [2](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1465324925006851#bib0002), [3](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1465324925006851#bib0003)\].

The manufacturing process of [CAR T cells](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/medicine-and-dentistry/chimeric-antigen-receptor-t-cell) is complex. It begins with [leukapheresis](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/medicine-and-dentistry/leukapheresis), a process to collect [peripheral blood mononuclear cells](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/medicine-and-dentistry/peripheral-blood-mononuclear-cell) (PBMCs) from patients. These PBMCs are cryopreserved within a day and transported to a manufacturing facility, where cryopreserved PBMCs undergo various steps for tisagenlecleucel production \[ [4](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1465324925006851#bib0004)\]. The final manufactured tisagenlecleucel product should meet approved commercial product specifications to ensure the quality such as safety, purity, identity, and potency. These specifications and threshold were initially determined by company and subsequently approved by regulatory authorities to ensure product quality ( [Table S1](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1465324925006851#sec0017)). However, it occasionally fails to meet them due to the nature of patient-derived cells with different properties as starting material and the complex manufacturing process. The final product that does not meet at least 1 of the commercial release specifications is referred to as “out-of-specification” (OOS) \[ [5](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1465324925006851#bib0005)\].

Since the approval of tisagenlecleucel, the primary goal has been to scale up manufacturing capacity and continuously improve manufacturing efficiencies. We have previously reported process improvements across manufacturing facilities, contributing to an improved commercial manufacturing experience of tisagenlecleucel worldwide \[ [\[6\]](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1465324925006851#bib0006), [\[7\]](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1465324925006851#bib0007), [\[8\]](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1465324925006851#bib0008)\]. The OOS rates for tisagenlecleucel declined progressively in the United States (US), Europe as well as in Japan over the years \[ [6](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1465324925006851#bib0006), [7](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1465324925006851#bib0007)\]. In Japan, OOS rates declined from 7.6% in 2019 to 3.7% in 2023 \[ [8](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1465324925006851#bib0008)\]. Although the frequency of OOS occurrence is decreasing, it is not possible to completely eliminate OOS occurrences owing to the characteristics of products. Patients for whom tisagenlecleucel is indicated are r/r patients with limited treatment options. Even an OOS product may have therapeutic effects due to the mechanism of action being unique to CAR T-cell therapy and may still be used at the physician’s discretion for patients with severe or life-threatening disease who require urgent treatment and have limited treatment options. For these reasons, OOS CAR-T products have been released in accordance with each country’s regulations for patients for whom it is difficult to wait for remanufacture and is required to use an OOS product. While no formal efficacy and safety analyzes of OOS tisagenlecleucel have been conducted to date, previous reports indicate that the efficacy and safety of OOS tisagenlecleucel appeared to be comparable to those of the in-specification tisagenlecleucel product that met all specifications \[ [\[9\]](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1465324925006851#bib0009), [\[10\]](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1465324925006851#bib0010), [\[11\]](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1465324925006851#bib0011), [\[12\]](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1465324925006851#bib0012), [\[13\]](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1465324925006851#bib0013)\].

The pathways for providing OOS products differ from one country to another and are supplied in accordance with the regulations of each respective country \[ [14](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1465324925006851#bib0014), [15](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1465324925006851#bib0015)\]. For instance, in the US, such products are provided via expanded access program \[ [14](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1465324925006851#bib0014)\]. In Japan, these products are currently provided and administered in a clinical trial. Therefore, we have initiated a prospective clinical trial for OOS products at all treatment sites where commercial tisagenlecleucel will be utilized. Herein, we report the results from a phase 3b clinical trial evaluating the safety and efficacy of OOS tisagenlecleucel treatment in patients with r/r DLBCL and r/r B-ALL in Japan \[ [15](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1465324925006851#bib0015)\]. This is the first report analyzing the clinical outcomes of Japanese patients treated with OOS tisagenlecleucel.

## Methods

### Study design

This is a single-arm, open-label, multicenter, prospective, interventional phase 3b study (NCT04094311) in patients who were consistent with the label indication (r/r [DLBCL](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/medicine-and-dentistry/diffuse-large-b-cell-lymphoma) and r/r B-ALL) \[ [15](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1465324925006851#bib0015)\]. For patients indicated for commercial tisagenlecleucel, [leukapheresis](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/medicine-and-dentistry/leukapheresis) is performed outside of the study, as per standard clinical practices. When manufacturing of the product is terminated due to reasons such as insufficient T-cell expansion or failure to meet safety-related specifications (eg, sterility, mycoplasma) after manufacturing process completion, no product is released. When OOS is confirmed by commercial release tests such as measurements of cell dose (total CAR+ viable T-cell count), percentage of viable T cells and transduction efficiency by [CAR](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/pharmacology-toxicology-and-pharmaceutical-science/chimeric-antigen-receptor) qPCR \[ [4](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1465324925006851#bib0004)\], the sponsor provides an OOS product risk assessment form for treating physicians. Physicians then assess the risk/benefit based on OOS character (eg, OOS type and parameters) and the patient’s need for the OOS product based on several factors, such as urgency of the treatment, availability of alternative treatment options, including remanufacturing of tisagenlecleucel and the patient’s [informed consent](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/medicine-and-dentistry/informed-consent) regarding potential risks. Patients are enrolled in the study only when the physician officially requested the OOS provision, and the eligibility criteria are met ( [Figure S1](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1465324925006851#sec0017)).

The study was originally initiated in Japan and Canada in 2019. However, Canada withdrew from the study in 2021 due to the availability of OOS tisagenlecleucel for Canadian patients through the managed access program. The study protocol was then amended in 2022 to solely focus on Japanese patients and reduced the follow-up period. The current analysis included data from Japanese patients who were enrolled in the original study protocol including 3 months of follow-up. This study evaluates the safety and efficacy of OOS tisagenlecleucel for commercial release in patients who were consistent with the label indication ( [Table S2](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1465324925006851#sec0017)) \[ [15](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1465324925006851#bib0015)\].

### Patient population

The key inclusion criteria included patients who had signed informed consent, OOS product had not been deemed to pose any undue risk, a serious or life-threatening disease or condition and repeat leukapheresis that was not clinically appropriate per the investigator.

The study followed the recommendations in the approved tisagenlecleucel package insert for lymphodepleting chemotherapy. Bridging therapy was permitted based on the investigator’s judgment. A single [intravenous infusion](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/medicine-and-dentistry/intravenous-drug-administration) of OOS tisagenlecleucel in suspension form was administered to patients following the same procedure as that for commercial tisagenlecleucel. Patients were followed up for 3 months after infusion.

### Outcomes

[Adverse events](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/pharmacology-toxicology-and-pharmaceutical-science/adverse-event) (AEs), including their type, frequency, and severity, as well as laboratory abnormalities, were carefully assessed and analyzed. Safety and [efficacy assessments](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/medicine-and-dentistry/efficacy-assessment) were performed as clinically indicated and not by a pre-set visit schedule. AEs were reported using the [Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/medicine-and-dentistry/meddra) (version 25.1) and Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (version 5.03). The severity of [cytokine release syndrome](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/pharmacology-toxicology-and-pharmaceutical-science/cytokine-release-syndrome) (CRS) was assessed according to the Lee criteria \[ [16](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1465324925006851#bib0016)\]. Grading of immune effector cell–associated [neurotoxicity syndrome](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/medicine-and-dentistry/toxicity-and-intoxication) (ICANS) adhered to the American Society for Blood and Marrow Transplantation consensus grading \[ [17](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1465324925006851#bib0017)\]. Efficacy assessments were performed by the investigators according to protocol-specified guidelines based on Lugano 2014 criteria in r/r [DLBCL](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/pharmacology-toxicology-and-pharmaceutical-science/diffuse-large-b-cell-lymphoma) \[ [18](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1465324925006851#bib0018)\] and NCCN 2013 v1 guidelines Cheson BD, et al., 2003 and Appelbaum F, et al., 2007 in r/r B-ALL \[ [\[19\]](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1465324925006851#bib0019), [\[20\]](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1465324925006851#bib0020), [\[21\]](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1465324925006851#bib0021)\], which were used in the JULIET and ELIANA studies \[ [\[22\]](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1465324925006851#bib0022), [\[23\]](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1465324925006851#bib0023), [\[24\]](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1465324925006851#bib0024), [\[25\]](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1465324925006851#bib0025)\]. For patients with r/r DLBCL, the overall response rate (ORR) was reported. ORR was defined as the percentage of patients who achieve a complete response (CR) and partial response (PR) as best response within 3 months following tisagenlecleucel infusion among patients that response assessments were performed, and data were available per study. For patients with r/r B-ALL, the overall [remission rate](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/medicine-and-dentistry/spontaneous-remission) was defined as the percentage of patients who achieve either complete remission or complete remission with incomplete hematologic recovery as best response within 3 months. However, only individual patient-level response data were reported owing to fewer patients. Owing to the 3-month follow-up, duration of response, progression-free survival, and [overall survival](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/pharmacology-toxicology-and-pharmaceutical-science/overall-survival) could not be estimated in both indications.

### Statistical analysis

The data were based on an interim analysis, with the data cut-off being May 8, 2023, for Japanese patients who were enrolled in the original protocol and completed the 3-month follow-up or discontinued the study. Safety was evaluated using the safety analysis set, which includes all patients who received an OOS tisagenlecleucel infusion. Efficacy was evaluated using the efficacy analysis set, which includes all patients who received an OOS tisagenlecleucel infusion and had at least 1 efficacy assessment post-baseline. Categorical data were presented as frequencies and percentages and continuous data as descriptive statistical measures. Data were analyzed separately for r/r patients with DLBCL and B-ALL.

## Results

Overall, 29 Japanese patients were enrolled in the original study protocol between December 2019 and May 2022 across 13 qualified sites in Japan, comprising 24 patients with r/r DLBCL and 5 patients with r/r B-ALL ( [Figure 1](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1465324925006851#fig0001)). In patients with r/r B-ALL, the majority of OOS tisagenlecleucel (4 of 5) were due to the formulation of a higher than approved cell dose range in the infusion bag. Although such products are categorized as OOS, the actual volume administered to patients was adjusted to ensure it remained within the approved cell dose by reducing the infusion volume. Therefore, actual administered components of such products were theoretically equivalent to those products meeting commercial specifications. On the other hand, major types of OOS observed in patients with r/r DLBCL were due to low [cell viability](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/medicine-and-dentistry/cell-viability) and low cell dose, and the patients were administered products containing cells of less [cell viability](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/pharmacology-toxicology-and-pharmaceutical-science/cell-viability) or less cell numbers than the approved specifications **.** Therefore, this paper further discusses OOS in patients with r/r DLBCL only. Individual data for patients with r/r B-ALL are summarized in the [Supplementary Table S3](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1465324925006851#sec0017).

Of the 24 enrolled patients with r/r DLBCL (n = 24), 23 received OOS tisagenlecleucel, while 1 patient discontinued before infusion due to progressive disease (PD). Of the 23 patients, 16 completed the 3-month follow-up and 7 discontinued during the follow-up period (death, n = 4 and withdrawal from the study, n = 3) ( [Figure 1](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1465324925006851#fig0001)). The median age of patients was 61 years (range, 33–72 years). Most patients had received prior stem cell transplantation with 52.2% (n = 12) and 3 or more prior lines of therapy, with 56.5% (n = 13) ( [Table 1](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1465324925006851#tbl0001)). The median time from the most recent relapse to OOS tisagenlecleucel infusion was 3.2 months.

The investigator then decided to infuse 2 OOS bags simultaneously, as the combined cell numbers were still within the commercially approved dose range. The patient achieved CR.

## Discussion

This initial report, which analyzes the clinical outcomes of OOS tisagenlecleucel administered to Japanese patients from a phase 3b trial, demonstrates that the OOS tisagenlecleucel infusion is feasible and safe. Although it is difficult to evaluate OOS, as whole population owing to every single OOS is different depending on the product used, the study patient outcomes, including incidence of CRS and ICANS, were in line with the data from both pivotal [clinical trial](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/pharmacology-toxicology-and-pharmaceutical-science/clinical-trial) (JULIET) and real-world studies (CIBMTR registry and SET-UP study) \[ [10](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1465324925006851#bib0010), [22](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1465324925006851#bib0022), [23](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1465324925006851#bib0023), [26](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1465324925006851#bib0026)\].

The most common type of OOS was low [cell viability](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/medicine-and-dentistry/cell-viability) in patients with r/r DLBCL. Despite this, the products in most cases still met the approved dose range criteria by CAR+ viable T cells. The response was observed regardless of the cell-viability percentage. In this study, no significant association between [cell viability](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/pharmacology-toxicology-and-pharmaceutical-science/cell-viability) and clinical outcomes were observed for patients treated with low viability OOS, which is consistent with earlier studies \[ [8](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1465324925006851#bib0008), [10](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1465324925006851#bib0010)\]. The second most observed type of OOS was low cell dose. Among the 8 cases of low-dose OOS, 3 had other unmet criteria simultaneously, including low cell viability, low [CAR expression](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/medicine-and-dentistry/receptor-expression), low transduction efficiency, and low release of IFN‐γ. For these low-dose OOS cases, the dose ranged from 0.167 to 0.527 × 108 CAR+ viable T cells, with variability ranging from near to extremely low end of the approved dose (0.6–6.0 × 108 CAR+ viable T cells). In the JULIET study, no apparent relationship was observed between the dose and exposure or response \[ [22](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1465324925006851#bib0022), [23](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1465324925006851#bib0023), [27](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1465324925006851#bib0027)\]. However, due to limited data towards the lower end of the dose range, the [dose response relationship](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/pharmacology-toxicology-and-pharmaceutical-science/dose-response-relationship) at doses below 1 × 108 cells could not be elucidated. In a real-world setting, data on the outcomes of OOS tisagenlecleucel due to doses lower than the approved dose are limited. In the real-world data from the United States, data from 3 low-dose OOS patients were reported \[ [10](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1465324925006851#bib0010)\]. Among these, the lowest dose infused was 0.37 × 108 cells; however, the clinical response was not reported. Although the lower threshold of the dose has not been clarified, the lowest dose with a responder in our study was in CR at 0.30 × 108 CAR+ T cells. For 1 patient (patient T), [apheresis](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/medicine-and-dentistry/apheresis) was performed again because the first manufacturing result was OOS, and the remanufactured product also resulted in OOS. The attempt of combined use of both bags within the approved dose range resulted in CR.

Our prospective study findings align with those of previous studies that reported comparable results for in-specification and OOS tisagenlecleucel in real-world settings. An analysis by the [Pediatric](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/medicine-and-dentistry/pediatrics) Real World CAR Consortium (PRWCC) revealed a similar safety and efficacy profile between in-specification and OOS tisagenlecleucel in paediatric patients with r/r B-ALL. No significant differences were observed in CR at day 28, 12-month [overall survival](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/pharmacology-toxicology-and-pharmaceutical-science/overall-survival) and event-free [survival rates](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/pharmacology-toxicology-and-pharmaceutical-science/survival-rate), or rates of CRS, ICANS and infections \[ [12](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1465324925006851#bib0012)\]. A recent study by Phillippis et al. \[ [13](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1465324925006851#bib0013)\] reported no significant differences in progression-free survival, overall survival, or toxicity (CRS and ICANS) of patients with r/r DLBCL between the in-specification and OOS tisagenlecleucel cohorts over a median follow-up of 19.1 months.

Another notable aspect is the identification of risk factors for OOS occurrences. Factors such as pretreatment with [bendamustine](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/pharmacology-toxicology-and-pharmaceutical-science/bendamustine) have been reported to contribute to OOS cases. Numerous studies have underscored the lymphodepleting capacity of [bendamustine](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/medicine-and-dentistry/bendamustine) and its impact on T-cell functionality for CAR-T therapies \[ [11](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1465324925006851#bib0011), [28](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1465324925006851#bib0028)\]. However, the number of patients with r/r DLBCL who had a history of bendamustine treatment (for 28 days or more) was minimal (n = 2) in our study. This prevented establishing any association with an increased risk of OOS products. In addition, we did not observe clear relationship between leukapheresis CD3+ T cell counts and OOS type.

While our findings provide valuable insights, they are limited by the small sample size, which reduces the analytical power. As this study included only patients opting for OOS treatment, comparisons with cases where OOS is not observed are challenging. Given the limited number of patients and the heterogeneity of OOS definitions, it is not possible to establish a specific correlation with the OOS type and the outcomes. Additionally, the brief follow-up period constrains data availability on the long-term efficacy and safety. Despite these limitations, the current study provides valuable data on the detailed actual administration of OOS product. The recent publication showed that approximately 75% of OOS tisagenlecleucel were infused in this study \[ [8](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1465324925006851#bib0008)\]. Our data can guide physicians in deciding whether to administer an OOS product, underscoring the need for an ongoing review of release criteria. Further long-term OOS data are being investigated in a separate study and are expected to be reported in the future.

## Conclusion

In conclusion, our findings affirm that the infusion of OOS tisagenlecleucel is a viable option, with no observed increase in toxicity and outcomes comparable to those of in-specification products. Although it is important to carefully determine OOS treatment based on the expected benefit-risk, the current study suggests that OOS tisagenlecleucel could be a consideration for patients who have limited treatment options.
