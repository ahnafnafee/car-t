## ABSTRACT

Tisagenlecleucel is approved for the treatment of relapsed/refractory (r/r) B cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia (B-
ALL) in patients up to age 25 years based on the results of a pivotal trial (ELIANA) in pediatric and young adult
patients. However, that trial did not include patients age <3 years because of the challenges posed by leukapheresis of very young and low-weight patients. Data on leukapheresis material and manufacturing outcomes among
patients age <3 years have been collected since the time of global regulatory approval. Here we report leukapheresis characteristics and manufacturing outcomes for tisagenlecleucel produced for patients age <3 years in US
and non-US commercial settings. Qualified patients with r/r B-ALL were age <3 years at the time of request for
commercial tisagenlecleucel, with manufacturing data starting after August 30, 2017 (date of first US Food and
Drug Administration approval). Leukapheresis and manufacturing outcomes data were stratified by age and
weight. CD3<sup>+</sup> cell count and CD3<sup>+</sup>/total nucleated cell (TNC) percentages were obtained from the leukapheresis
material; leukocyte subpopulations were obtained via quality control vials. Of the 146 tisagenlecleucel quality
control batches analyzed for CD3<sup>+</sup> cell count and CD3<sup>+</sup>/TNC%, 86 batches (84 patients) were from US sites and 60
batches were from non-US sites. The median patient age and weight were 1.2 years and 10.4 kg at US sites and
1.5 years and 10.5 kg at non-US sites. Globally, 137 of 146 batches (94%) were manufactured within specifications
across 16 countries. Among tisagenlecleucel batches manufactured in the United States between 2017 and 2021,
there was a trend toward increasing CD3<sup>+</sup> counts, CD3<sup>+</sup>/TNC%, and manufactured dose of chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T cells; there was no difference in median days of collection by patient age or weight. Globally, a trend
toward 1 or more potential additional collection days was observed for patients weighing 10 kg. Leukapheresis
and tisagenlecleucel manufacturing in pediatric patients with r/r B-ALL age <3 years, including infants (<1 year),
and low weight are feasible. As global experience with leukapheresis and patient identification for CAR-T cell therapy increased over time, a corresponding improvement in tisagenlecleucel manufacturing success has been
observed. Clinical outcome data for these patients are currently being explored.

## INTRODUCTION

Tisagenlecleucel (Kymriah) is an anti-CD19 chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T cell therapy [1,2] approved by the US
Food and Drug Administration (FDA) on August 30, 2017, and
the European Medicines Agency on August 23, 2018, for
the treatment of patients up to 25 years of age with B cell
acute lymphoblastic leukemia (B-ALL) that is refractory or in
second or later relapse. The single-arm Phase II ELIANA trial

(ClinicalTrials.gov identifier NCT02435849) demonstrated the
efficacy and safety of a single infusion of tisagenlecleucel in
pediatric and young adult patients with relapsed or refractory
(r/r) B-ALL [2]. Among patients who received tisagenlecleucel
infusion and had at least 3 months of follow-up, the overall
remission rate (ORR) was 81% (95% CI, 71% to 89%), all of which
were minimal residual disease-negative. With a longer median
follow-up of 60.1 months, tisagenlecleucel has continued to
show a high ORR of 82%, with a median duration of remission
of 43 months and median overall survival not reached [3].

## METHODS

## Patient Eligibility Criteria

Qualified pediatric patients with r/r B-ALL were age
<3 years at the time of request for commercial tisagenlecleucel, with manufacturing data starting after August 30, 2017
(date of first US FDA approval). Patient age was defined as age
at the date of the patient order form submission. The majority
of patients had tisagenlecleucel manufactured at the Morris
Plains, New Jersey manufacturing facility; other sites of tisagenlecleucel manufacturing included facilities in Switzerland,
France, and Japan. Tisagenlecleucel was administered at the
patient’s respective authorized Kymriah treatment center in
the United States or other countries (Table 1). The cutoff for
leukapheresis collection and manufacturing data was December 31, 2021.

products included formulated CAR-T products that did not
meet at least one of the approved commercial specifications.

## Guidelines for Optimizing Leukapheresis

Guidelines for optimizing leukapheresis in this patient population are based on patient body weight, regardless of age;
timing of collection; and peripheral CD3<sup>+</sup> and/or absolute lymphocyte count (ALC) and leukemia disease burden prior to cell
collection, as described previously [11,12].

## RESULTS

## Patient Characteristics

One hundred forty-six batches of commercial tisagenlecleucel (Kymriah) for patients with r/r B-ALL who were age
<3 years, had undergone leukapheresis, and had cells sent to
Novartis with an order for manufacturing commercial tisagenlecleucel were included in the analysis. A total of 86 batches
were analyzed for 84 patients at US centers, and 60 batches
were analyzed at non-US centers. The number and proportion
of approved and certified Kymriah treatment centers globally
that sent manufacturing batches from patients age <3 years
have increased since 2017/2018 (Figure 1A); in both the
United States and globally, there was an upward trend in the
number of batches sent for manufacturing during the 4-year
period (Figure 1B, Table 1).

The median patient age was 1.2 years (range, .3 to 3 years)
in the US centers and 1.5 years (range, .7 to 2.9 years) in non-
US centers, and median body weight was 10.4 kg (range, 5.8 to
20 kg) and 10.5 kg (range, 6.2 to 18 kg), respectively. Of the
146 batches that proceeded to manufacturing, 41 batches
were from patients age <1 year and 105 were from patients
age 1 to <3 years (Figure 1C); 67 batches were from patients
weighing 10 kg and 79 were from patients weighing >10 kg
(Figure 1D). The prevalence of batches from patients age <1
year was higher in the US centers than in non-US centers
(Figure 1E), whereas the prevalence of batches from patients
weighing 10 kg was similar in US and non-US centers
(Figure 1F).

The median duration of leukapheresis for the patients was
1 day. Fifty-six percent of the batches were collected in a single
day of leukapheresis, 35% were collected on 2 days of leukapheresis, and 9% were collected on 3 or more days of

## Leukapheresis Characteristics

Among the 146 batches, the median incoming CD3<sup>+</sup> cell
count was 1.29 £ 10<sup>9</sup> CD3<sup>+</sup> cells (range, 1.64 £ 10<sup>8</sup> to
5.98 £ 10<sup>9</sup>), and the median CD3<sup>+</sup>/TNC% was 42.1% (range,
1.52% to 100%). The CD3<sup>+</sup> cell count and CD3<sup>+</sup>/TNC% in collected leukapheresis material tended to increase from 2019 to
2022 in US patients (Figure 3A,B) but remained consistent in
non-US patients over this period (Figure 3C,D). CD3<sup>+</sup> cell
counts and CD3<sup>+</sup>/TNC% were comparable between patients
weighing 10 kg and those weighing >10 kg at collection
(Figure 3E,F). Leukapheresis material from 12 batches (9 from
US patients and 3 from non-US patients) did not meet the
study specifications—6 batches did not meet the minimum
specification for TNC count and met the specifications for CD3<sup>+</sup>
cell count and CD3<sup>+</sup>/TNC%, and 6 batches either did not meet
the minimum specifications for CD3<sup>+</sup> count or CD3<sup>+</sup>/TNC%—but
proceeded to manufacturing regardless. In all samples (US and
non-US combined), incoming leukapheresis median percentages were 13.6% (range, 0 to 96.6%) CD19<sup>+</sup> B cells and 52.6%
(range, 1.4% to 97.2%) CD3<sup>+</sup> T cells (Figure 4). Among US
patients, analysis of leukocyte populations showed that the
percentage of B cells (blasts and/or healthy B cells) in the leukapheresis material decreased over time (Figure 4A), whereas
the proportion of T cells remained consistently high
(Figure 4B). For non-US patients, B cell and T cell percentages
in the leukapheresis material remained consistent over time
(Figure 4C,D). Overall, the median B cell and T cell content in
the incoming leukapheresis material was well balanced
between the patients weighing 10 kg and those weighing
>10 kg (Table 2).

## Manufacturing Outcomes

The median manufactured cell dose was 2.65 £ 10<sup>6</sup> CARpositive viable T cells/kg (range, .23 to 4.98 £ 10<sup>6</sup> [2.75 £ 10<sup>6</sup>
for patients 10 kg and 2.51 £ 10<sup>6</sup> for patients >10 kg]),
median cell viability was 91.5% (range, 66.7% to 98.3% [92.2%
for patients 10 kg and 90.9% for patients >10 kg]), and
median CAR-positive expression was 12% (range, 2.1% to 37.2%
[12.1% for patients 10 kg and 11.8% for patients >10 kg]).
When we examined the changes in final product characteristics over time, we observed a substantial increase in the
median dose of CAR-positive viable T cells per kilogram, the
median percentage of viable cells over time, and the median
percentage of CAR-positive cells in final products manufactured in the US (Figure 6A-C) and outside the US (Figure 6D-F).

## DISCUSSION

Clinical trials of tisagenlecleucel have demonstrated high
ORR and an acceptable safety profile among pediatric and

young adult patients with r/r ALL age ≥3 years at screening
[2,3,14]; however, pediatric patients age <3 years were
excluded because of concerns about the inability to collect
and expand an adequate number of T cells from young and
low-weight patients. Since regulatory approval, real-world
data for tisagenlecleucel in patients age <3 years have
increased in recent years [5,7,10,15-22]; however, patient
numbers remain small, leaving an ongoing need to investigate
this population to further demonstrate the safety and feasibility of leukapheresis and tisagenlecleucel manufacturing in
patients age <3 years.

With the approval of tisagenlecleucel by the US FDA and
health authorities outside the United States in pediatric and
young adult patients with r/r ALL up to age 25, the ability to
treat patients with r/r ALL age <3 years has been available
since late 2017 for the United States and later dates for other
countries [12,23]. A retrospective analysis of real-world data
from the Pediatric Real World CAR Consortium among 14 pediatric patients age <3 years with r/r ALL who received commercial tisagenlecleucel between 2017 and 2020 demonstrated
the safety and feasibility of manufacturing for this patient population, as well as good outcomes and safety profile [7]. Novartis and the Center for International Blood and Marrow
Transplant Research (CIBMTR) presented outcomes data at

CAR-T therapy is a multistep process that requires training
and coordination of a multidisciplinary team involved in leukapheresis collection, cell processing, timing and coordination
with the manufacturing site, and administration of the CAR-T
product to achieve the best possible clinical outcomes [11].
Logistical parameters, such as the timing and location of leukapheresis and patient access, must be considered in planning
the overall collection strategy and evaluating the patient’s
suitability for CAR-T therapy. In smaller patients, securing vascular access for leukapheresis and obtaining sufficient T cell
yields can be challenging. Optimization of leukapheresis collection requires the washout of prior medications that may
have a negative effect on the patient’s T cells, the use of predictive algorithms to maximize collection, and cryopreservation
or shipment of the leukapheresis product within 24 hours of
collection.

Tisagenlecleucel manufacturing outcomes also have
improved since market approval, with an increasing cell dose
and percentage of CAR<sup>+</sup> T cells in the final product and increasing manufacturing success rate [6,24-27]. Analysis of patient
selection, earlier collection with fewer lines of therapy including hematopoietic stem cell transplantation, T-cell “stemness,”
and leukemia burden at the time of collection may provide a
better understanding of the roles of these factors on
manufacturing success and clinical outcomes, resulting in
more effective CAR-T therapy. It is also possible that some children are not undergoing leukapheresis at their physician’s discretion based on clear guidelines about cell count
requirements for collection. These guidelines for starting apheresis material cell counts include a CD3<sup>+</sup> cell count ≥1 £ 10<sup>9</sup>,a
TNC count ≥2 £ 10<sup>9</sup>, and a CD3<sup>+</sup>/TNC% ≥3% for tisagenlecleucel
[11].
